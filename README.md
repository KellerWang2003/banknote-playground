# Banknote Playground

A design tool for previewing lenticular security threads on banknotes. Upload 2 to 5 frames per banknote and tilt it back and forth to see the thread animate.

```bash
npm install
# Copy .env.example to .env.local and fill in the publishable key.
npm run dev
```

## What it does

- **Projects:** create (just a name), open, rename, duplicate and delete. **Demo** opens a temporary preview of two sample banknotes, playing: Bird watermark (a bird portrait in almost white paper, only a hint flat and clear tilted or held to the light, beside the flapping bird on a windowed thread, and a back where the bird shows through turned over) and Star thread (Partial reveal: a gold star spins through five frames on up/down tilt but only the middle three side to side, and the two outer frames add a small star between the big ones). Its playlist swings them up and down, then side to side, to 30°. Mixed angles stays out of it until it is known to be printable. It is built in code (`src/lib/demo.ts`, its artwork drawn in `src/lib/demoArt.ts`), so it ships with the app. Its edits are discarded when you exit, and it never appears in the project list. Enter a username to open its shared workspace. Projects and uploaded images autosave to Supabase and are available on other browsers and devices. Browser storage keeps recovery copies while offline. Anyone who knows a username can open and edit its workspace.
- **Units:** lengths read in mm, cm, inches or points. Click the unit at the end of any length field to switch every length at once; the choice is kept in this browser and never changes a value, since everything is stored in mm. A unit typed after a number (6.14in, 6.14", 15.6cm, 442pt) converts without switching, and scrubbing or the arrow keys step round numbers of the unit (`src/lib/units.ts`).
- **Banknotes:** a project holds any number of banknotes, like artboards. Each has its own name, size (portrait or landscape), design (an uploaded image for each side; without one, plain paper), thread and frames.
- **Thread:** vertical or horizontal, thickness 3 to 6 mm, position across the note, optional background colour, and individually editable windows. The Windows switch turns windows on (continuous when off, when the card says the sponsor, Oberthur, requires them) and shows the first one ready to adjust; + adds one in the widest free stretch. There is no edit mode: picking a window on the canvas edits it there (draw more on the thread, move them, or drag their ends), and clicking elsewhere on the banknote, the empty canvas or Escape ends it. Sections snap to 0.5 mm. Meanwhile every window's length shows on it (or beside it, where it doesn't fit) and the picked window's gaps to its neighbours, or its distance from the banknote's edge, beside it: each is a field to scrub or type, for exact sizes and places. Delete removes the selected window. Complete frame images stay upright, fill the thread's thickness at their own aspect ratio, and repeat to the banknote edge. Image gap sets the space between copies, defaulting to 0 mm; gaps show the thread background or the banknote underneath.
- **Frames:** up to 5 per banknote, images or colours. Each frame has a length on the timeline; its share of the total is its share of the cycle. Replace, recolour, reorder or delete from a frame's ⋯ menu, or drop images on a banknote.
- **Tilt:** drag a banknote or the empty canvas (both ways) to tilt every banknote up to ±90° (edge-on), or press Play (a tap of Space plays or pauses) to sweep from flat (0°) through the full Cycle angle and back to flat. Flat shows the middle frame by default; drag the pin on the frame strip in the thread editor's Lens to move it (start or middle of any frame). For Partial reveal on one tilt, drag the ends of the band on that strip to hide more or fewer frames. **1 → 5**, in the ⋯ menu on the Lens title, sets whether frames run top to bottom (the default for new projects) or bottom to top, and left to right or right to left. **Cycle** (degrees for one full pass through the frames) and **Playback settings** (fade between frames, speed, motion curve, and the playlist of moves, where ⏸ Hold pauses flat for its seconds and ⇄ Flip turns every banknote over to its other side, as F does) are shared by every banknote in the project. Past one cycle the frames loop.
- **Watermark:** + on a banknote's row adds one, under the banknote as a layer beside its thread. Upload one image. A watermark is thinner paper letting light through, so only brightness counts and it only ever gets lighter: the image's light parts are the thinner paper, so it glows where the image is light, and its background (or transparency) is plain paper. **Invert** makes the dark parts glow instead, so a black logo glows. **Auto levels** fits how strongly each part shows to the image, so JPEG specks in the background stay plain paper and a faint image comes up; the slider under it sets that by hand, as in Photoshop's Levels: where the watermark starts (black handle), its midtones (grey) and where it is strongest (white). Moving a handle turns Auto off; a new image starts on Auto. It shows in the banknote's own colours, under the thread, brightening the design (dark print stays dark, as ink blocks light): only a hint flat, clear as the banknote tilts (from 30°, no light needed), and clear even flat held to the light; Depth sets how strongly it shows at its clearest. On the banknote it drags like the thread: press it and drag to move it. Picking it, on the banknote or from its layer, shows the banknote flat with the watermark clear and its box: drag it, drag a corner to resize it (proportions kept, 0.5 mm steps) or nudge it with the arrow keys; X, Y, W and Depth set it exactly. A click elsewhere, the empty canvas or Escape ends placing, and Delete removes it. Duplicate keeps it.
- **Magnetic ink:** + on a banknote's row adds one, a layer beside its watermark (SICPA's SPARK Flow PRIME look). Upload one image for its shape: whatever is opaque on transparency, or whatever stands out from an opaque background, takes the ink, so a JPEG's specks stay clear and a faint scan prints solid; **Invert** inks round the shape instead. **Flat** is its colour seen flat and **Tilted** the colour it shifts to as the banknote turns away, either way (all of it from 30°). A bright bar sits across its middle flat and rolls off its edge by 20° on one tilt: **Bar rolls on** ↕ (a level bar) or ↔ (an upright one), and **Bar width** sets how wide it is. **Realistic** lights it as metal flakes instead, from the same settings, under a light from above as in a room: the lamp's reflection with a soft glow round it, towards the top of the shape flat and in its middle as the top tips back 10°, the colour shifting across the shape as each part is seen at its own angle, and a sparkle as it turns (drawn with WebGL; a browser without it keeps the usual look). It is drawn over the design and the watermark, under the thread. It drags, places and is removed as the watermark is (its box, corners, arrow keys, X, Y and W; Escape ends placing, Delete removes it). Duplicate keeps it.
- **Arc-en-Ciel:** + on a banknote's row adds one, a layer between the watermark and magnetic ink (Oberthur's colour bands that slide as you tilt: an offset line screen in three or more colours, embossed in register by a blind intaglio plate). It starts as a disc; upload one image for its shape, read as magnetic ink's is, or turn on **Invert** to put the bands round the shape instead. **Bands from** sets where the bands start: **Edge** (inward from the edge of each separate shape, so every petal or letter gets rings of its own), **Holes** (outward from holes in the shape; greyed out when it has none), **Point** (rings from points placed on the canvas) or **Line** (along a line drawn on the canvas, straight on past its ends). **Colours** lists three or four colours in the order the bands run from where they start, repeating: drag a row's grip, or use the arrow keys on it, to reorder; + adds a fourth and × takes one out, down to three. **Band width** is one band's width (0.3 to 5 mm) and **Moves on** the tilt that slides the bands, ↕ or ↔: half a band each way by 20°, one band in all, outward as the top tips away or the right side turns away. While it is placed, Point's starts show as fuchsia dots, and Line as a fuchsia line with a dot at each end and a hollow dot at each bend: drag a dot to move it (up to the box's own size off it) or the line to move it whole, double-click in the box to add a point (up to 8) or on the line to bend it (up to 8 points in all), and click a dot to pick it for the arrow keys or Delete (never the last point or the line's ends). Bands finer than the screen's pixels fade to their colours' average. **Realistic**, last on the tab and off by default, draws the embossed ridges themselves instead, 0.4 mm apart along the tilt that moves the bands, lit by magnetic ink's lamp above your head: each ridge's two slopes carry the colours half a band either side, so flat every spot mixes two neighbouring colours, and tilting across the ridges lets the slopes facing you win until, by 20°, only they show and the bands sit where the slide takes them. The ridges are round, with a soft shadow down each groove and a broad sheen, and the print looks printed rather than exact: the colours run into each other, the ink is a little uneven, the print sits slightly out of register with the ridges, and the paper is grainy and a little cockled. The paper inside a rubber band round the shape (its holes and bays too) is embossed white; ridges finer than the screen's pixels show their average. It is drawn with WebGL over the design and the watermark, under magnetic ink and the thread (a browser without it shows the bands at rest, Realistic or not), only on the front, and exports the same way. It places, drags and is removed as the other placed layers are. Duplicate keeps it.
- **Back:** every banknote has a back with its own design (**Front** and **Back** side by side on the Banknote tab, each an uploaded design; without one, plain paper). **Front | Back** at the top of the canvas, or F, turns every banknote over, side to side; again turns them back. A **Flip** in Playback settings' moves does the same as playback comes to it (not in the thread editor). The back shows no thread, no Arc-en-Ciel and no magnetic ink, only the watermark, which is in the paper: it shows through the other way round, at the other side, and can be dragged there too. Picking the thread, Arc-en-Ciel or magnetic ink turns the banknotes back to their fronts.
- **Rotate:** **Rotate left** and **Rotate right**, beside Front | Back at the top of the canvas, or Shift+R and R, turn every banknote a quarter turn on screen about its own middle, with everything on it (not in the thread editor). The banknotes still tilt as they would turned in the hand: a drag tilts them, and moves the thread, watermark, magnetic ink and windows, the way the pointer goes, as arrow keys do. It is only a way of looking: nothing is saved, and the banknotes are upright again when the project is opened. Exports come out turned the same way.
- **Held to the light:** the lamp on the tilt pad, or a tap of L, holds every banknote up to the light, which shows the watermark clearly at any tilt; the design and the thread look the same. Hold L to peek at the other light. Playback's ☀ Hold to light move stays flat while the light fades in, holds (seconds per move) and fades out; recordings keep the light too.
- **Pasting images:** with an image or an SVG on the clipboard (a copied file, a screenshot, a browser's Copy image, Figma's Copy as PNG or Copy as SVG), point at any card or button that uploads an image and press ⌘/Ctrl+V: the banknote's Front and Back cards, the + on a side without a design, a frame's Tile pattern card, the watermark's and magnetic ink's Image, or the timeline's + (several at once, a frame each). With the pointer over none, the one with the keyboard's focus takes it. It works inside Figma's code layers too: pointing at a card brings the keyboard to the app, so the paste lands there rather than on Figma's canvas. Figma's own copy of a layer (⌘C) only pastes in Figma, and a notice says to copy it as PNG or SVG instead.
- **Continuous check:** for a banknote whose background is an uploaded design (at trim size, no bleed), **Continuous check** on its Banknote tab checks that the design carries on where its edges meet its neighbours' on the print sheet, top into bottom and left into right (`src/lib/continuous.ts`). With the banknote picked, its outline and name label step aside and the canvas shows copies of the design around it where its neighbours sit, as strong as the banknote where they meet it so you can see whether the colour carries across, fading out further away, amber bars where an edge breaks, and the **Cut tolerance** (1 mm by default) as a dashed line inside each edge; **Drift** previews a cut landing that far off, the neighbours' art coming in. The **Checks** drawer at the tab's foot lists the breaks, and also warns when the paper under the watermark or within 2 mm of the thread isn't light (L* 80 or more), as the brief asks. Turned over, it checks the back's design. A check only: nothing is drawn in exports.
- **Lens effect** (one per banknote, or one per thread section with Mixed angles; edited in the thread editor): Horizontal, Vertical, Diagonal 45°, Partial reveal on one tilt, or Custom angle, named by the lens ridges. A lens plays one sequence of frames; each tilt runs through it at its own cycle, and the lens angle follows from the two (tan angle = ↕ cycle ÷ ↔ cycle, `src/lib/lens.ts`). Partial reveal computes the slow tilt's cycle so the outer frames never show at any tilt up to 90°, with a Wobble allowance for a hand that drifts onto the fast tilt (flat shows the middle frame). Custom sets each tilt's cycle, or the lens angle and lens cycle. Frames a tilt never reaches get an eye-off badge with ↕ or ↔ on the timeline and in the panel, and a tilt map shows the frame at every tilt. Clicking the tilt map flips it to the print: the thread's frames, magnified, cut into strips under the ridges and woven together as they would print (`src/lib/print.ts`), with the strips seen at the current tilt lit. When side tilt responds, Playback can sweep ↕, ↔ or both.
- **Thread pattern** (`src/lib/pattern.ts`): the thread is a pattern of sections within one repeat (Repeat, in mm) that tiles the whole thread. Each section is an area of the repeat (along in mm, across as a share of the thread's width) with a frame track (which banknote frame each strip of its print shows; a shorter story is padded) and, while **Mixed angles** is on, its own lens; with it off every section plays the banknote's lens. A banknote from before patterns is one section over the whole thread and plays exactly as before. Presets (`src/lib/patternPresets.ts`): Single lens, Split band, Alternating and Chevron lock. The layout rules are checked live and only warn (`src/lib/patternRules.ts`): every window shows each tilt wherever the print lands, one rest frame, frames only some sections show sit under ridges near their tilt's axis, one strip count, minimum sizes, few ridge angles, no gaps. The print isn't registered to the banknote, so **Offset** previews one landing along the thread and **Shuffle** tries another, listing the windows that miss a tilt there; it is remembered per browser and never saved in the project. The canvas, export and print draw each section with its own frame.
- **Export:** the Export button (top right of the canvas, or beside the banknote's name when one is selected) saves one playback loop as MP4 or GIF: Banknotes are ticked in a list, side by side by default with an option for separate files; resolution (720p to 4K for MP4, 360p to 1080p for GIF), frame rate, loops (MP4) and background (transparent for GIF) are adjustable. It uses the current Cycle and Playback settings. The watermark follows the tilt and Hold to light moves as on the canvas, magnetic ink shifts colour and rolls its bar as it does there (lit the same way when Realistic), and with the lamp left on it is clear for the whole video; banknotes left turned over export their backs, banknotes left rotated export rotated, and Flip moves turn them over as on the canvas (a playlist that leaves them on their other side plays twice, so the video ends on the side it began). The banknotes are redrawn on a canvas (`src/lib/exportRender.ts`) to match the live view, then encoded in the browser (`src/lib/exportMedia.ts`, loaded only when exporting).
- **Canvas:** every banknote sits on one canvas, handled as Figma's. Scroll, middle- or right-drag, or drag with Space held to pan, pinch or ⌘-scroll to zoom (or the − and + buttons, with the zoom between them, which a click takes to 100%, about true size; ⛶ fits every banknote), drag a banknote or the empty canvas to tilt, drag a banknote's label to move it, or its thread, watermark or magnetic ink to move that on the banknote. The pointer outlines what a click would pick (a banknote, its whole thread, its watermark or its magnetic ink), and what is picked stays outlined.

## Cloud setup and Vercel

The backend is deployed to **General playground** (`swovjhbaoxlxodakauxo`). No email setup or Supabase Auth accounts are required. Use the same username on another device to open the same projects. Usernames ignore case and accept 2–32 letters, numbers, hyphens, or underscores.

For Vercel, import this repo, keep the Vite preset, and add `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` from `.env.local`. Build with `npm run build`; output is `dist`. Set the variables for each Vercel environment you intend to use and redeploy after changing them. No database password or service-role key belongs in Vercel or the browser.

Production is the Vercel project `banknote-playground`, deployed from `main`, at https://banknote-playground.vercel.app. The older https://of-playground.vercel.app serves the same deployment, with no redirect: a browser keeps its username, settings and offline copies per address, so anyone who started there keeps them.

Existing browser projects appear under **Import browser projects** in the workspace's project list. Import them from the same browser and origin where they were created; the original copies are retained.

See [backend and save behavior](docs/SUPABASE.md) for schema, deployment, recovery, and verification details.

## Editor layout

A full-screen canvas with floating panels:

- **Bottom left:** the tilt card. The ↕ and ↔ fields take exact angles, and each lock keeps the pad and canvas drag off that axis. The chevron above them opens the tilt pad (folded away by default; the browser remembers): drag anywhere on it to tilt every banknote, up/down vertically, side to side horizontally, with the centre flat; it stays where you let go. ↺ beside the chevron or a double-click on the pad returns to flat, and arrow keys nudge the pad when focused (Shift for 5°). The lamp beside the chevron (or L) holds every banknote up to the light.
- **Top left:** back to Projects, the project name, and the collapsible Banknotes list (double-click to rename, + to add, duplicate, delete). Each banknote opens to its layers: Security thread, then Watermark, Arc-en-Ciel and Magnetic ink once added (the + on the banknote's row lists the layers it doesn't have yet).
- **Bottom:** play/pause, with Playback settings and the # frame-number toggle stacked beside it, always; Record a move, at the end of Playback settings' list of moves, opens the tilt pad for a drag that becomes the playlist (Escape cancels). With a banknote selected, its frames follow as clips on a track, with + after the last one: drag a frame's right edge to change its length, drag to scrub, scroll to move along, ⌘-scroll to zoom. The playhead shows where in the cycle the tilt is.
- **Right, while a banknote is selected:** name and Export, then a tab per part, as the list has them: **Banknote** (size, the front's and back's backgrounds), **Thread** (placement with a compact Windows card, artwork, and **Lens effect**: the lens's basic controls as in the thread editor, with its preview (frames seen, tilt map, or with Mixed angles the whole print) in a **Preview** drawer at the panel's foot, and Offset and Shuffle when a section is shorter than the repeat), **Watermark** once added (position and width, its image with Invert and levels, and depth), **Arc-en-Ciel** once added (position and width, its image with Invert, where the bands start, their colours, the band width and the tilt that moves them, and Realistic) and **Magnetic ink** once added (position and width, its image with Invert, the Flat and Tilted colours, the tilt its bar rolls on and the bar's width, and Realistic): every placed layer's panel starts with where it sits. The open tab is the part picked: clicking a part on the canvas or in the list turns to its tab, and a tab picks that part. Picking another banknote shows that banknote, never the part picked before, and tilting the selected banknote by dragging it keeps the tab. Later features such as intaglio get their own layer and tab.
- **Thread Studio** (the thread editor: **Edit in Thread Studio** just under the selected thread on the canvas, or double-click a thread): full screen over the canvas. Top: back, the banknote's name, Mixed angles, Repeat, Ridges and undo/redo. Left: the presets, each with a ridge picture, and the Sections list (double-click to rename, + to add in the largest gap or else cut the selected section in two, duplicate, delete). Middle: the thread magnified over two or three repeats with its real artwork at the current tilt, the repeat's ends dashed, each section outlined and, with Ridges, hatched at its ridge angle. Drag on empty thread to draw a section (it takes the look and name of the section beside it), drag a section to move it or its edges to resize it (snapping to 0.5 mm along and twelfths across); a click selects, a double-click on empty thread fills that gap (a section in line with it runs on over it, or a new one takes its look), Delete removes (selecting the next section), Escape deselects and then goes back. Right: the selected section's Area and Frames (pick the frame each strip shows), the Lens (the section's own with Mixed angles, otherwise the banknote's, marked Shared) and the Checks, where a click on a problem selects its section and Fill fills every gap the same way. Bottom: the tilt pad and the playback bar, the timeline following the selected section.

## Undo and redo

Undo/redo buttons sit beside Projects in the editor, and in the thread editor's top bar. Use ⌘/Ctrl+Z to undo, ⌘/Ctrl+Shift+Z to redo, or Ctrl+Y to redo. In the thread editor they take back only that banknote's steps and the playback every banknote shares; a step on another banknote stops them there, and the canvas's undo still reaches it. History covers project names, banknotes, artwork, thread settings and patterns, lens and timeline edits; the print offset is a preview and isn't undone. Continuous drags and scrubs are grouped into one step; text fields retain native text undo while focused. Restored edits autosave normally. Each project keeps up to 100 steps for the current session, including temporary demos.
